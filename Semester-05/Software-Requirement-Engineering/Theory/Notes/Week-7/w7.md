## **1. What is Requirements Elicitation?**

**Definition (important for exam):**

Requirements elicitation is the process of gathering, discovering, and defining the needs, desires, and constraints of stakeholders for a software system.

Let's understand it in simple words.

Suppose a university wants to develop an Online Course Registration System.

Before developing it, the software engineer needs to know:

- What do students need?
- What do teachers need?
- What does the administration need?
- What problems do they currently face?
- What features should the system provide?

Collecting this information is called **Requirements Elicitation**.

**Remember these three words:**

1. Gathering – Collecting information.
2. Discovering – Finding hidden needs and problems.
3. Defining – Clearly writing the requirements.

**Exam tip**

If the question asks, "What is Requirements Elicitation?", write the definition and explain these three points.

## **3. Requirements Workshops**

**Definition (exam-ready):**

A requirements workshop is a planned meeting in which stakeholders work together for a specific period to collect, improve, and revise requirements.

### **Simple example**

Suppose a university wants to develop a registration system.

The development team invites:

- Students
- Teachers
- Administration staff
- Project managers

They sit together and discuss the system's requirements.

One student says: "We need an option to drop courses."

A teacher says: "We need to view student class lists."

The administration says: "We need to manage course registration deadlines."

All these requirements are discussed and recorded.

This meeting is called a Requirements Workshop.

### **Important role: Recorder**

A workshop must include a recorder.

**Recorder:** A person responsible for writing down the ideas, discussions, and requirements shared by participants.

### **Benefits of Requirements Workshops**

Your slide (page 6) identifies three important benefits:

1. **Faster decisions:** Stakeholders resolve conflicts and make decisions quickly.
2. **Shared alignment:** Everyone receives the same information, reducing misunderstandings.
3. **Early risk detection:** Technical and operational problems are identified before development starts.

**Exam question**

**Q: Why are requirements workshops useful?**

Answer: They help stakeholders make faster decisions, understand requirements together, and identify risks early.

## **4. Surveys and Questionnaires**

Very important

This is one of the most important topics in your Week 7 lecture.

### **4.1 What is a Survey?**

**Definition (exam-ready):**

A survey is a method of gathering information from a group of people by asking questions.

**Example:**

A university wants to know how students feel about its online registration system.

It sends a Google Form to 500 students and asks questions about:

- Registration problems.
- Required features.
- System speed.
- Ease of use.

This is a survey.

### **4.2 What is a Questionnaire?**

**Definition (exam-ready):**

A questionnaire is a written or digital set of questions designed to gather information from an individual.

**Example:** A Google Form containing 10 questions about a university registration system.

### **Difference between Survey and Questionnaire**

| Questionnaire                      | Survey                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| A set of questions.                | A complete information-gathering process.                  |
| Collects answers from individuals. | Distributes questions and collects and analyzes responses. |
| It is a tool.                      | It is a method/process.                                    |

**Easy trick:**

- Questionnaire = Questions.
- Survey = Complete process.

### **4.3 Common Types of Surveys**

Your lecture mentions four common types:

1. **Customer Feedback:** Measures customer satisfaction and experience.
2. **Market Research:** Checks customer demand and tests new ideas.
3. **Employee Engagement:** Collects feedback about workplace culture and job satisfaction.
4. **Academic and Social Research:** Studies human behavior, demographics, and public opinions.

Example: A university asking students about their satisfaction with online classes is Academic Research.

## **5. Types of Questionnaire Questions**

Must prepare for MCQs and scenarios

There are three main types in your lecture.

### **Type 1: Closed-Ended Questions**

**Definition:**

Closed-ended questions provide limited answers, usually Yes/No.

Example:

**Q: Have you used our university portal?**

- Yes
- No

Another example:

**Q: Did you find the required course?**

- Yes
- No

**Important points:**

- Easy to answer.
- Easy to count.
- Easy to compare.
- Answers are limited.

### **Type 2: Multiple-Choice Questions**

**Definition:**

Multiple-choice questions provide several options, and the respondent selects an answer.

Example:

**Q: Which device do you use to access the university portal?**

- A. Smartphone
- B. Laptop
- C. Tablet
- D. Desktop computer

Another example:

**Q: Which feature do you use most?**

- A. Dashboard
- B. Messaging
- C. Reports
- D. Account settings

**Important points:**

- Provides different options.
- Easy to analyze.
- Helps identify user preferences.

### **Type 3: Open-Ended Questions**

**Definition:**

Open-ended questions allow respondents to answer in their own words without fixed options.

Example:

**Q: What problems do you face while registering for courses?**

Possible answer:

"I face timetable clashes and slow registration."

Another example:

**Q: What features would you like to add to the university portal?**

**Important points:**

- Provides detailed answers.
- Helps discover hidden needs.
- Allows users to express their opinions.
- Answers can be difficult to analyze.

### **Comparison of all three types**

| Type            | Answer format | Example                    |
| --------------- | ------------- | -------------------------- |
| Closed-ended    | Yes/No        | Have you used the portal?  |
| Multiple-choice | Given options | Which device do you use?   |
| Open-ended      | Own words     | What problems do you face? |

## **6. Designing a Good Questionnaire**

According to page 12, the lecture gives five steps.

## **Questionnaire Design Flow**

1. **Define Objective**

   Decide what information you want to collect.
2. **Select Stakeholders**

   Identify the people who will answer the questions.
3. **Write Clear Questions**

   Use simple and understandable language.
4. **Choose Answer Type**

   Select closed-ended, multiple-choice, or open-ended questions.
5. **Pilot Test and Finalize**

   Test the questionnaire with a small group before sending it.

### **Guidelines for a Good Questionnaire**

Remember these five points from the slide:

1. Use simple and specific language.
2. Ask only one thing in one question.
3. Avoid biased or leading questions.
4. Keep questions connected to system goals.
5. Test with a small group before sending.

**What is a leading question?**

A question that pushes the respondent toward a particular answer.

Bad example:

"Don't you agree that our portal is excellent?"

Good example:

"How would you rate our university portal?"

## **7. Analyzing Survey Responses**

This topic is shown in the diagram on page 13.

After collecting questionnaire responses, the development team must analyze them.

### **Survey Analysis Pipeline**

1. **Collect Responses**

   Gather all answers from stakeholders.
2. **Group Similar Needs**

   Put similar answers together.
3. **Find Repeated Problems**

   Identify common problems reported by users.
4. **Convert into Requirements**

   Turn user problems into clear requirements.
5. **Validate with Stakeholders**

   Confirm that the requirements are correct.

### **Advantages of Surveys**

1. Covers many stakeholders quickly.
2. Useful for geographically scattered users.
3. Responses can be quantified.
4. Supports feature prioritization.

### **Limitations of Surveys**

1. No immediate clarification.
2. Poor questions produce weak data.
3. Response rate may be low.
4. Hidden needs may be missed.

## **9. Benefits of Stakeholder Involvement**

Why should we involve stakeholders during requirements engineering?

According to page 15, there are five important benefits.

1. **Correct Needs:** Real user problems are captured.
2. **Less Rework:** Problems are found before design and coding.
3. **Better Acceptance:** Users feel ownership of the system.
4. **Priority Clarity:** Important features are identified first.
5. **Risk Discovery:** Conflicts and constraints are found early.

**Easy exam answer:**

Stakeholder involvement helps the development team understand real needs, reduce rework, improve user acceptance, identify priorities, and discover risks early.

### **When and How to Involve Stakeholders?**

According to page 16, stakeholder involvement follows five stages:

1. **Identify:** Who matters?
2. **Plan:** When should we meet?
3. **Elicit:** Collect their needs.
4. **Validate:** Confirm the accuracy of requirements.
5. **Manage:** Handle changes.

Different stakeholders need different elicitation techniques.

Examples from the slide:

- End users → Observation / Prototype.
- Managers → Interview / Workshop.
- Large groups → Survey.

### **10. Insufficient User Involvement**

**Definition:**

Insufficient user involvement means users are not properly consulted, or their feedback is ignored during software development.

What happens when users are not involved?

| Effect               | Meaning                                |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Wrong requirements   | System solves the wrong problem.       |
| Missing exceptions   | Important unusual cases are ignored.   |
| Poor usability       | Users struggle to complete tasks.      |
| Late change requests | Cost and time increase.                |
| Low acceptance       | Users do not accept the final product. |

**Important exam statement:**

A system can be technically correct but still fail if real users are not involved.

# **11. Case Study – University Course Registration System**

High-priority exam preparation

This is the practical activity on pages 20–22. Your teacher can ask a similar scenario in the exam.

### **Understand the scenario**

A university wants to develop an Online Course Registration System.

The system should allow:

- Students to log in.
- View available courses.
- Check prerequisites.
- Register or drop courses.
- View their timetable.
- Teachers to view class lists and schedules.
- Administration to manage courses, seat limits, prerequisites, and deadlines.

Current problems:

- Long registration queues.
- Timetable clashes.
- Incorrect prerequisite checking.
- Delayed registration confirmation.

Let's solve the case study step by step.

### **Q1. Identify Target Respondents (Stakeholders)**

**Answer:**

1. Students.
2. Faculty members.
3. Academic administration staff.

These are the main respondents because they use or manage the system.

### **Q2. What is the Questionnaire Objective?**

**Answer:**

The objective is to collect information about user needs, current problems, required features, usability, performance, security, and system constraints.

### **Q3. Create at least 10 Questionnaire Questions**

Your lecture specifically requires:

- 3 Closed-ended questions.
- 2 Multiple-choice questions.
- 3 Open-ended questions.

Here are 10 exam-ready questions.

**A. Closed-Ended Questions**

1. Do you currently face problems during course registration? (Yes/No)
2. Do you want automatic timetable clash detection? (Yes/No)
3. Should students receive registration confirmation? (Yes/No)

**B. Multiple-Choice Questions**

4\. Which device do you use for course registration?

- Smartphone

### **Q4. Extract Requirements from Responses**

Now understand the most important practical concept.

Suppose users give the following feedback:

| User feedback                               | Derived requirement                                      |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Students face timetable clashes.            | The system shall automatically detect timetable clashes. |
| Students want quick confirmation.           | The system shall send registration confirmation.         |
| Students cannot check prerequisites easily. | The system shall display course prerequisites.           |
| Students want secure accounts.              | The system shall provide secure login.                   |

### **Q5. Classify Requirements**

There are three categories in your case study.

**1. Functional Requirements**

These describe what the system must do.

Examples:

- The system shall allow students to register for courses.
- The system shall check course prerequisites.
- The system shall detect timetable clashes.
- The system shall allow students to drop courses.

**2. Non-Functional Requirements**

These describe the quality or performance of the system.

Examples:

- The system shall respond quickly.
- The system shall protect student information.
- The system shall be easy to use.
- The system shall remain reliable during registration periods.

**3. User Requirements**

These describe what users need from the system.

Examples:

- Students want to view available courses.
- Teachers want to view class lists.
- Administration staff want to manage course offerings.

### **Q6. Challenge Question**

Suppose 80% of students report that course registration becomes very slow on the first day.

**Question: What requirement should be derived? Is it Functional or Non-Functional?**

Correct answer:

Requirement: The system shall maintain fast response times and handle high numbers of users during peak registration periods.

Classification: **Non-Functional Requirement.**

Why?

Because it describes system performance, not a particular function.
